4 QUICK GUIDES
▣+

Lock down your accounts

Passphrases, password managers, and multi-factor authentication.

Use a unique passphrase for each account. A password manager can create and remember strong passwords. Turn on multi-factor authentication, keep recovery options current, and never share sign-in codes.

Read the full CISA guide ↗
⌁+

Spot a phishing message

Check the sender, the ask, and where a link really goes.

Check the full sender address, unexpected urgency, and the real destination of a link. Don’t open surprising attachments or sign in from a message link. Contact the organization through its official app or a website you type yourself.

Read the full FTC guide ↗
♡+

Recognize relationship scams

Know the patterns before money or trust is on the line.

Someone you meet online may build trust, then ask for travel, emergency, or investment money. Don’t send money, gift cards, or crypto to someone you haven’t met in person. Talk it over with someone you trust and reverse-search profile photos.

Read the full FTC guide ↗
◉+

Pause a payment request

Why gift cards, crypto, and wire transfers deserve extra care.

Scammers impersonate trusted organizations, create an urgent problem or prize, then demand a hard-to-reverse payment. Pause and verify using a number or app you find independently. Don’t pay through links or contact details in the request.

Read the full FTC guide ↗

If you think you’ve been scammed

01
Stop contact

Don’t reply, click, or send more money.

02
Secure accounts

Change affected passwords; call your bank using its official number.

03
Report it

Save messages and receipts, then report to the right agency.
